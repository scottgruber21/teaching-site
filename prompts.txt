KOREAN WAR LESSON

Anchor Prompt: The "Unfinished War" Lesson
Context: We are building the "Unfinished War" interactive lesson, the centerpiece of Scott Gruber's teaching portfolio. This module acts as a digital alternative to a traditional classroom presentation, designed for 9th-grade World History.

Pedagogical Objective: Students (and viewing administrators) must understand that the Korean War's lack of a formal peace treaty directly created the modern geopolitical landscape.

The Narrative Arc (Sourced from Lesson Plan):

The Hook (Satellite Slider): Comparing 1953 nighttime imagery ("Line of Light") with modern political borders.

The "Why" (Policy): Explaining "Containment" as the driver for US involvement.

The Grinding Stalemate: Visualizing the shift from 1950 movement to 1951-53 trench warfare.

The "Aha!" Moment (Armistice vs. Treaty): Defining the Armistice as a "Pause Button" rather than a "Stop Button."

The Legacy (Evidence Hunt): Interactive cards showcasing the "Miracle on the Han River" (SK) vs. "Centralized Dictatorship/Nuclear Program" (NK).

The Closure (Diplomat’s Desk): A text-based exit ticket for "proposing a peace treaty."

Interactive Features:

Svelte Components: Must be used for the Satellite Slider and Evidence Hunt to ensure reactivity.

Teacher’s Voice (Anime Avatar): A persistent scaffold that updates via scroll-trigger, providing "Teacher-Talk" context and pedagogical rationale (e.g., "I start here because...").

Content Source of Truth: All historical facts and framing must align with the provided Korean War Lesson Plan.docx and Korean War Handout.

UX Priority: The experience must be "Guided Discovery." It shouldn't feel like a textbook; it should feel like an investigation. High-contrast "Ed Tech" visuals, rounded UI, and a clear "Skip" button for administrators are mandatory.
